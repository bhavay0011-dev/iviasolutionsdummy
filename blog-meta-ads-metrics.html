Reading a Meta Ads Report: The Numbers That Matter

How to read a Meta Ads report without getting lost: CPM, CTR, hook rate, frequency, CPA and ROAS, what each one tells you and which numbers to act on.

Read the Report as a Funnel

Every Meta ad goes through the same steps: it is shown, it stops the scroll, it gets a click, and the click turns into a sale or lead. Each step has a metric. When results drop, find the step that broke instead of changing everything at once.

1. Delivery: CPM and Frequency

  • CPM (cost per 1,000 impressions) is what you pay to be seen. It rises with competition, around festive seasons, sales events and with very narrow audiences.
  • Frequency is how many times the average person saw your ad. When frequency climbs and CTR falls together, the audience is tired of the creative.

High CPM alone is not a problem if the rest of the funnel converts. Judge it alongside cost per result.

2. Attention: Hook Rate and Hold Rate

For video ads, two simple ratios tell you whether the creative works:

  • Hook rate = 3-second video plays ÷ impressions. Low hook rate means the first seconds are not stopping the scroll.
  • Hold rate = ThruPlays (or 15-second views) ÷ 3-second plays. Low hold rate means people stop but leave before the message lands.

These are not default columns. Build a custom metric or calculate them in a sheet.

3. Interest: CTR (Link) and CPC

  • Use CTR (link click-through rate), not "CTR (all)". The "all" version counts likes, profile taps and "see more" clicks that never reach your site.
  • CPC (cost per link click) follows from CPM and CTR. Improve the creative and CPC usually falls without touching the budget.

4. Conversion: Conversion Rate, CPA and ROAS

  • Landing page views vs link clicks. A big gap means people click but the page loads too slowly, so they leave before it appears.
  • Conversion rate after the click is mostly about the landing page and the offer, not the ad.
  • CPA (cost per acquisition) is the number to manage for lead generation.
  • ROAS is the number to watch for e-commerce, but always next to your margin. A ROAS that looks healthy can still lose money on low-margin products.

Check the Attribution Setting

Meta reports conversions inside an attribution window, by default a mix of click-through and view-through. View-through conversions count people who saw the ad but did not click. Add the attribution setting column and compare click-only results with your store or CRM data. If Meta claims far more sales than your store received, trust the store.

The test that settles arguments: compare your total revenue (MER = total revenue ÷ total ad spend) with and without a campaign running. Platform numbers can overlap; your bank account does not.

What to Do When a Number Moves

  • Hook rate down: test new openings in the first seconds of the video.
  • CTR down, frequency up: refresh the creative or widen the audience.
  • Clicks fine, conversions down: check the landing page, the offer, stock and tracking.
  • CPM up across all ads: usually seasonal competition. Hold steady unless CPA breaks your target.

Key Takeaways

  • Read the report as a funnel: delivery, attention, interest, conversion.
  • Track hook rate and hold rate for video creative.
  • Use link CTR, not CTR (all).
  • Judge ROAS against margin, and check results against your own store data.
  • Fix the step that broke instead of changing everything at once.
