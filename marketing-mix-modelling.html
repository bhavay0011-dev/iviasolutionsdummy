Know what really sells.

Marketing mix modelling (MMM) uses your own sales history to work out how much each channel actually adds, after seasonality, pricing and promotions are taken out. We use it to move budget from channels that are saturated to the ones where the next rupee still earns, then check every move with an experiment.

How MMM works

Three ideas behind every model.

An MMM looks at months of weekly sales next to everything that might have moved them. It separates what each channel added from what would have happened anyway, then learns two things about every channel: how long it keeps working, and when it stops paying back.

01

Base vs incremental

Part of your sales comes from your brand, word of mouth, seasonality and repeat customers. The model separates that base from what each channel adds on top. Only the added part counts as a channel's return, which is why MMM numbers are usually lower, and more honest, than platform dashboards.

02

Carryover

Ads keep working after the week they run. People remember, search later, buy next payday. The model learns this decay, called adstock, for every channel.

03

Diminishing returns

The first rupees reach the most interested people. Each extra rupee reaches someone a little less likely to buy, so returns flatten. The model finds where each channel's curve bends.

How we run it

Our MMM playbook.

01

Data audit & collection

Ideally two to three years of weekly sales and spend by channel, plus prices, promotions, seasonality and other drivers. If the data is not ready, we say so and build it first.

02

Build the model

A Bayesian model built with open-source frameworks such as Google Meridian, Meta Robyn or PyMC-Marketing, with carryover and diminishing returns for every channel.

03

Validate & calibrate

Accuracy checked on weeks the model has never seen, then calibrated with lift or geo-holdout tests so channel effects match real experiments.

04

Optimise & plan

Response curves feed a budget optimiser: what to cut, what to scale and what a bigger or smaller budget would do, shown as ranges rather than false precision.

05

Test, refresh, repeat

Recommended shifts roll out as controlled tests. Results flow back into the model, which is refreshed as new data arrives.

A statistical model that links your sales over time to everything that drives them: spend on each channel, prices, promotions, seasonality and more. It estimates how much each channel adds on top of the sales you would have made anyway. It uses aggregated weekly data, not individual user tracking, so cookie loss and privacy changes do not break it.

Attribution follows clicks and views of individual people and gives credit to the touchpoints it can see, so it misses offline sales, cross-device journeys and slow-burn brand effects, and every platform tends to over-claim. MMM works top-down from your total sales, so it sees every channel at once but cannot judge a single ad. We use both, plus experiments to settle disagreements.

Ideally two to three years of weekly sales and spend by channel. Google's Meridian guidance, for example, recommends at least two years of weekly data for regional models and three years for national ones. Spend also needs to have varied over time. If you are not there yet, we start with clean tracking, structured spend tests and geo holdouts, and build the model as history builds up.

Yes, and this is where MMM is most useful. Total sales across your website, marketplaces and offline can be the outcome the model explains, and marketplace ads sit in the model as channels alongside Meta and Google. If you have sales by state or city, we can build a regional model and run geo tests.

It is an estimate, so we always show a range, not a single magic number. We check accuracy on weeks the model has not seen, calibrate it against lift or geo tests, and treat every recommendation as a test to confirm before scaling. Where the model is unsure, we say so.

The first model usually takes a few weeks once the data is collected. After that we refresh it on a regular schedule, typically monthly or quarterly, and after any major test, so it keeps learning from what actually happened.
